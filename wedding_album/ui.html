3. Recognition settings

The defaults were tuned on a real 25-photo shoot. Change one at a time and re-analyze to see the effect.

Is this the same person?

How alike two faces must be to be treated as one person.

0.20 merges more0.70 splits more
Raise it if two different guests were merged into one person. Lower it if the same person shows up as several entries below. Default 0.363, the figure recommended for this face model.
How certain must a face be?

The confidence needed before something counts as a face at all.

0.20 finds more0.95 finds fewer
Lower it to catch guests in profile or turned away from the camera. Raise it if statues, posters or blurry background shapes keep appearing as people. Default 0.50 deliberately errs towards finding too much: an extra entry you mark "Not a person" costs a click, while a missed relative costs a photo.
When are two photos the same shot?

Used both to flag duplicates and to group poses together.

10 = only identical140 = loose
Measured on your own photos: 48 was the same pose twice, 78 was the same outfit in a different pose, 126 was two unrelated photos. Default 60 calls a repeat a repeat while keeping a genuinely different pose.
What makes a photo "good" — quality weights and two more rules
Sharpness

Is the subject in focus?

The strongest sign of a keeper: a soft or shaken photo can't be rescued. Raise it if blurry shots are still getting picked. Default 0.35, the highest of the five.
Exposure

Too bright, too dark, or just right?

Penalises washed-out whites and lost shadow detail, which cannot be recovered either. Raise it for outdoor shoots in harsh sun. Default 0.18.
Contrast

Does the photo have depth, or look flat?

Separates a punchy frame from a hazy one. Lower it if you shoot deliberately soft or pastel work and don't want it penalised. Default 0.18.
Colour

How rich are the colours?

Favours vivid saris, flowers and decor. Lower it to near zero for black-and-white or muted film looks, which would otherwise score badly through no fault of their own. Default 0.17.
Grain

Speckly noise from shooting in low light.

Cleaner is better, so this counts against grainy frames. Kept lowest because it measures coarsely on these files and is more of a tiebreaker than a verdict. Default 0.12.
These are relative

Only the balance between them matters.

They are rescaled automatically, so they need not add up to anything in particular. Doubling one is the same as halving the other four. Scores are also relative to this collection: 100 means the best of these photos, not perfect.
When is a child the subject?

How big a child's face must be to send the photo to the Kids page.

0.02 almost any0.30 close-ups only
A toddler wandering through the back of a family portrait should leave it under Family. Raise it if background children are pulling photos into Kids. Default 0.08, roughly a face filling a twelfth of the frame.
How many people make a group?

Unlabelled people needed before a photo counts as a group shot.

Only applies to people you haven't identified. Once you label them, the photo moves to Family, Friends or wherever they belong. Default 3.

4. Who is who

Tell it each person's relationship, not their identity: sisters, in-laws and cousins all belong to Family. Use Not a person for statues, posters and framed photographs. Clicking a role refiles photos straight away, with no re-analysis.

5. Album preview
