Three things that apply to every step

PICK ANYEvery photo carries a 📌 button, and every step carries the rejected pile — everything removed so far, grouped by the step that removed it, with the reason. You can claim any frame at any point, before or after it was rejected.

NO INTERFERENCEYour picks go into a separate set. They do not rejoin the funnel, do not change the ranking, and cannot alter which frames become selects. The engine's run is left exactly as it was and delivered alongside yours, in Manually_Selected/. The counter in the header is deliberately separate from the "still in" count for the same reason.

PARAMSEvery removal step exposes the actual thresholds the engine uses, with a note under each on how to choose it. Defaults are the values in the code today. Nothing is hidden behind "smart".

Parameters — duplicates

How alike counts as a duplicate
config.DUPLICATE_HASH_THRESHOLD  default 60
60
5 only identical files140 merges different poses

How to choose: this is a Hamming distance on a 256-bit perceptual hash — how many of those 256 bits differ between two frames. 0 means pixel-identical; 256 means nothing in common.
Important: most advice online quotes a threshold of about 12, but that is for a 64-bit hash. This app uses 256 bits, so those numbers are four times too small here.
Use 20–40 to catch only true re-shoots of the same frame. 60 is the default and catches a burst where someone barely moved. Above 100 you start merging genuinely different poses and will lose photographs you wanted.

Why duplicates can run before the quality analysis

MEASUREDDuplicate detection needs the photo decoded. Decoding 40 photos takes 2.67s; decoding and measuring every metric takes 3.01s — the extra metrics cost 11% because the decode dominates. So there is no cheap duplicates-only pass worth building. The app scans once here, caches, and every later step is instant, including stepping backwards.

Parameters — exposure

diagnose.BLOWN_MAJOR  default 0.10
10%
1% very strict40% only the worst

How to choose: the fraction of the frame that is pure white (value 250 or above). That detail is gone permanently — no editing brings it back.
A little clipping is normal and often fine: a window, a bright sky, sun on a white saree. 10% means a tenth of the frame is dead white, which usually means the shot is lost. Set 4–6% if you are strict about highlights; 20% if your venue was very bright and you would rather judge by eye.

How to choose: average brightness of the whole frame, on a 0–255 scale. Above this it reads as washed out overall — not just hot highlights, but the entire photograph too bright.
202 is high on purpose, so only genuinely overexposed frames trip it. A bright outdoor ceremony can legitimately average 180–190. Lower it to 190 only if you see washed-out frames surviving.

Parameters — focus

diagnose.SOFT_PERCENTILE  default 22
22%
0 remove none50 remove half the batch

How to choose: this is a percentile, not a sharpness value. At 22 the softest 22% of this batch is called out of focus.
Why relative and not absolute: Laplacian variance has no universal "sharp" number — it depends on the subject, the lens and the resolution. A macro of a ring and a wide hall shot measure completely differently while both being perfectly sharp.
The consequence to understand: this always removes roughly that percentage, even if every frame is sharp. On a flawless batch it still cuts the softest fifth. Set it to 0 to disable, or lower to 10 if your focus hit rate is good.

9 photos at the 22nd percentile
diagnose.SLIGHTLY_SOFT_PERCENTILE  default 42
42%

How to choose: off by default. Everything between the 22nd and 42nd percentile is flagged "slightly soft" — 8 of your 40 — which is information, not a verdict. A slightly soft candid can still be the best photograph of the moment. Leave it off and let the score cut at step 9 handle these.

Sharpness measurement scales
vertical/metrics.SHARPNESS_SCALES  1.0, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125
fixed — do not change

Why this exists and why it is locked: sharpness is measured at four image sizes and the lowest result is used, so a frame must look sharp at every scale.
This is the fix for a real defect. Grain is high-frequency detail, so plain Laplacian variance read grainy frames as 12× sharper than they were, and they took every top slot while being described as "crisp detail". Downsampling averages grain away but leaves real detail, which cut the inflation to 1.4×. Removing a scale brings the bug back.

Parameters — subject and faces

diagnose.FACE_SOFT_RATIO 0.35 & FACE_SOFT_PERCENTILE 25
0.35
0.05 only extreme1.00 face must beat frame

How to choose: the face must be softer than this fraction of its own frame's sharpness and fall in the softest 25% of faces in the batch. Both have to agree before the fault is reported.
Why both: the frame ratio alone misfires — on your photos the face-to-frame ratio runs 0.01 to 6.74 with a median near 1.3, so faces are usually sharper than their frames. The percentile alone is just as bad: it always condemns the softest quarter even when every face is sharp.
0.35 sits in a clean gap in your data — the 9 flagged frames have faces at 1–27% of frame sharpness, with the next value at 45%. Raising it towards 0.6 starts catching sharp faces.

8 photos at 0.35
diagnose.FACE_DARK_RATIO 0.68 & FACE_DARK_ABSOLUTE 95
0.68
0.30 only severe backlight1.00 any dimming

How to choose: catches backlighting — the classic bright window behind the couple leaving their faces dark. The face must be dimmer than this fraction of the frame's brightness and below 95 of 255 absolutely.
Why this matters more than it looks: a whole-frame exposure reading misses backlighting completely, because the average looks fine. Only comparing the face against its surroundings finds it.
0.68 means the face is at roughly two-thirds the frame's brightness. Lower it to 0.5 to catch only severe cases.

What building this needs — two models, not one

MODEL 3Expressions. emotion-ferplus from the ONNX Model Zoo. Takes a 64×64 grayscale face crop, returns a 1×8 array of scores across neutral, happiness, surprise, sadness, anger, disgust, fear, contempt. Runs on OpenCV's existing DNN engine — no new dependency, just another file in models/. Trained on Microsoft's FER+, a relabelling of FER2013 where ten people tagged each image.

MODEL 4Eyes closed is separate. It is not one of the eight classes, so the expression model cannot answer it. YuNet gives five landmarks — two eye centres, nose tip, two mouth corners — with no eyelid points, so the usual eye-aspect-ratio trick is impossible. Needs a 68-point landmark model or a dedicated open/closed classifier.

About 2–3 hours each, plus download and verification.

Two cautions before asking for this

Expression is not emotion. The model reads the shape of a face, not what the person felt. A polite smile and genuine delight are identical to it. I would label the output "smiling / neutral / reacting" rather than "happy", because the stronger claim is not supported.

Accuracy will disappoint. FER2013 images are small, mostly frontal and web-sourced. Yours are 6000×4000 with faces at angles in temple lighting, and frontality already reads below 0.45 on 17 of 40 frames. A turned head is precisely where expression models fail.

Parameters — grain and clarity

diagnose.GRAINY_PERCENTILE 78 & GRAINY_MARGIN 1.25
78%

How to choose: a frame is called grainy if its noise is above this percentile of the batch and at least 1.25× the batch median.
Why both conditions: a percentile alone misfires badly when a batch has little spread. With most frames at near-identical noise, the cut-off lands on that shared value and flags everything — it tagged 17 of 28 on an early test before the margin was added.
Grain is usually acceptable in reception and night coverage; it is the price of a usable shutter speed. Leave this off unless you have a surplus of photographs.

How to choose: the standard deviation of brightness across the frame. Low means flat — haze, a grey sky, heavy fog, or a badly lifted shadow.

Design note

DATA EXISTSNo new model needed. face_count is already measured on every photo by the existing YuNet probe, and Candidate.face_count carries it through. Only the banding, the quotas and this screen are new.

The band boundaries line up with the engine's own GROUP_MIN_PEOPLE = 3, so this step agrees with how wedding_album already splits solo, couple and group sections.

Two limits worth knowing

It counts faces, not people. A guest turned away from the camera, half behind someone else, or cut by the frame edge is invisible to the detector. Large groups are routinely undercounted — your biggest frame reports 9 faces and almost certainly holds more.

It does not know who is in the photo. "Two people" could be the couple or two cousins. Telling them apart needs identity clustering, which wedding_album already does with the SFace model and relationship labels — that is a much bigger feature than this step, and a separate decision.

How to choose: same 256-bit hash distance as step 3, but looser. Step 3 asks "is this the same photograph?" at 60; this asks "is this the same moment?" at 100.
The gap between the two is deliberate: frames 60–100 apart are not duplicates but do belong to one setup, so they compete with each other rather than with the whole shoot.
Your batch gives 35 moments from 40 frames at this setting, which is high because the sample skipped through the shoot. A continuous run groups far more tightly. Lower it to 60–80 if different poses are being merged.

How to choose: picks are taken round-robin — the best of the strongest moment, then the best of the next, and only once every moment has contributed does it come back for second-best.
Why that matters: taking the top 6 scores outright returns six frames of whichever pose was lit best. That is one photograph six times, not a shortlist. This step is the difference between a shortlist and a pile.
If you ask for more selects than there are moments, the extras come from second-best frames of the strongest moments.

shortlist.pick(avoid_major_faults=True)
on by default

How to choose: leave on. Within each moment, clean frames are offered before flawed ones — but flawed frames are held back, not excluded. If you ask for 6 selects, you get 6; on a difficult batch the least-bad frame is the honest answer, and its reason line will name the fault rather than hide it.

Design note

NEW SCREENThe journey is the point of the redesign: you can see exactly why 39 photographs did not make it, which step took each one, and click back to any decision that looks wrong.

Below this would sit the full ranking table from v0.02, for when you want more than one photograph.

Two sets, delivered side by side

ENGINEThe numbered selects, 01_ to 06_, are the funnel's own answer. Nothing you do by hand changes them — not the ranking, not the ordering, not which frames won their moment. That run stays reproducible and explainable.

YOURSManually_Selected/ holds whatever you claimed, with original filenames and no numbering, because they were not ranked against anything. The journey below splits them into the two ways a frame can get there: kept before it was removed, or added back from the pile afterwards.

This matters most on the percentile steps. Focus takes the softest 22% of the batch whether or not those frames are genuinely soft, so on a well-shot wedding it will discard usable photographs simply because something had to be in the bottom fifth. The pile is how you retrieve them without touching the engine's logic.

Save the selected photos

Choose where they go and what the folder is called. The engine's selects and your own picks are written side by side, never merged. Your originals are never moved or altered — copies are written to the destination.

Destination

Where to save

How to choose: the default puts the album inside the shoot folder, which is what the existing album tool does and what organizer.album_dir() expects. It keeps the selection next to its source, so moving the shoot moves the album with it.
Pick Beside the shoot folder when you will export more than one ceremony and want to compare them side by side. Avoid Desktop for anything you intend to keep.

Folder name
organizer.next_version_name()  suggests BestImages_v1

How to choose: BestImages_v1 is the engine's own suggestion and the one I would take. The _v1 matters: the tool counts existing versions and offers the next free number, so a second run never overwrites the first. That is what lets you change a threshold, export again, and compare the two.
Worth knowing about your shoot: ~/Desktop/candid marriage/ already contains a BestImages_v1. Saving there would suggest BestImages_v2 instead. 2_Muhurtham/ has none, so _v1 is free.
Use a dated or descriptive name when the folder is leaving your machine — a client receiving "BestImages_v1" learns nothing from it, whereas "Muhurtham_Selects" explains itself.

How to choose: the first four are on by default and I would leave them. The notes and the CSV cost a few kilobytes and answer "why this one?" months later when nobody remembers.
