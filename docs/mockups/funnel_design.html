The keep button, on every step

NEWFrom step 3 onward every photo carries a Keep button. Pressing it protects that photo from every later step — it cannot be removed as a duplicate, as unusable, as weak, or by the one-per-moment rule.

The count of kept photos sits in the header throughout, and they are listed on the final screen. This is the escape hatch: if you disagree with the engine about one frame, you say so once and it stops arguing.

Design note

BUILT & TESTEDShipped in v0.03. Only the screen and the manual override are new.

No EXIF to read — all 40 files report orientation: 1, including the sideways ones. Rotation is inferred by running the face detector at four quarter turns and taking the most confident. A frame with no face is deliberately left alone, so a sideways shot of the venue or the rings will not be corrected automatically.

No two frames in these 40 are alike enough to count as duplicates. Loosen the threshold below if you expected some.

60

Hamming distance on a 256-bit perceptual hash. Lower is stricter. At 60 nothing matched; above about 100 genuinely different poses start merging and you lose photographs you wanted.

Read this one carefully

The frame scoring higher (71.8) is proposed for removal, because it is grainier and its partner is sharper on the face. Score alone is the wrong rule for duplicates, which is why pairs are shown side by side rather than resolved automatically.

Why duplicates can come before analysis

MEASUREDDuplicate detection needs a perceptual hash, which needs the photo decoded. Decoding 40 photos takes 2.67s; decoding and measuring every quality metric takes 3.01s. The extra metrics cost 11%, because the decode dominates.

So there is no cheap duplicates-only pass worth building. The app scans once on entering this step — about half a second across 7 cores — caches it, and every later step is instant, including stepping backwards.

What building this actually needs

MODEL 3Expressions. emotion-ferplus from the ONNX Model Zoo is the viable offline candidate. It takes a 64×64 grayscale face crop and returns a 1×8 array of scores across eight classes: neutral, happiness, surprise, sadness, anger, disgust, fear, contempt. It runs through OpenCV's existing DNN engine, so no new dependency — just another file in models/. Trained on Microsoft's FER+, a relabelling of FER2013 where each image was tagged by ten people.

MODEL 4Eyes closed is a separate problem. It is not one of the eight classes, so the expression model cannot answer it. YuNet gives five landmarks — two eye centres, nose tip, two mouth corners — with no eyelid points, so the usual eye-aspect-ratio trick is impossible. This needs either a 68-point landmark model or a dedicated open/closed eye classifier.

Estimated work: 2–3 hours for expressions, 2–3 more for eye state, plus download and verification.

Two honest cautions before you ask for this

Expression is not emotion. The model reports the shape of a face, not what the person felt. A polite smile and genuine delight look the same to it. I would label the output "smiling / neutral / reacting" rather than "happy", because the stronger claim is not supported.

Accuracy will be worse than the demos suggest. FER2013 images are small, mostly frontal and web-sourced. Your photographs are 6000×4000 with faces at angles, in temple lighting, sometimes partly turned away — and frontality already reads below 0.45 on 17 of 40 frames. A turned head is exactly where expression models fail.

My recommendation: build eyes-closed first. A blink ruins a photograph unarguably, and the judgement is objective. "Flat expression" is taste, and taste is what the keep button is for.

Why this is a slider and not a rule

My first design removed every frame carrying any minor fault. On your real batch that left 8 photos out of 40 — far too harsh.

The cause is Head turned away, which fires on 18 of 40. In candid wedding work someone looking away is the genre, not a defect. A blanket rule would have discarded nearly half the shoot.

BUILTThis is the round-robin selection already in the engine, finally visible. Frames are grouped by how alike they look, then one is taken from each group before any group gives a second.

Without it you get six frames of whichever pose was lit best — one photograph six times. This step is the difference between a shortlist and a pile.
